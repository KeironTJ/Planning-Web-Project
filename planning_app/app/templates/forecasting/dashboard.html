AI Tools

Order intake and shipped-output forecasting, analysis, and model configuration

Purpose and goal

AI Tools will connect order intake to shipped output. Project 1 forecasts incoming orders, their value, and verified volume; Project 2 uses forecast intake and the existing backlog to predict shipped value and volume over 12–16 weeks.

The toolset is being developed to support the whole process through the browser: prepare data, explore patterns, compare models, produce forecasts, and review their accuracy as actual results arrive. Forecasts will support business decisions alongside the order book and planners' judgement.

What we want to understand

Incoming orders

How many new orders are expected, and what value and verified quantity will they bring?

Intake is measured at customer order receipt. The original-value source, quantity basis, and usable history still need verification.

Shipped output

How much value and verified quantity will actually ship over the next 12–16 weeks?

Shipment date determines output timing. Existing backlog, amendments, cancellations, and partial shipments must be accounted for.

Weekly unit demand

How much quantity is currently due by required ship-by week?

This is a supporting planning measure, not labour hours or realised shipped output. UOM and quantity definitions need validation.

Forecast results will cover customer groups and the company total, using the existing planning groups by default, with Epicor groups as an alternative.

From data to a useful forecast

  1. Prepare: verify historical sources, classify products, and check data quality.
  2. Explore: examine trends, seasonal patterns, customer activity, and product mix.
  3. Experiment: configure features and models, tune parameters, and compare against simple baselines using historical forecasts.
  4. Forecast: review predicted intake and shipped value and volume, uncertainty, and exports.
  5. Monitor: compare forecasts with actuals, track accuracy and bias, and decide when to retrain.

Available tools

Data readiness and model classification are the available tools. No historical training dataset or forecasting baseline has been produced.
