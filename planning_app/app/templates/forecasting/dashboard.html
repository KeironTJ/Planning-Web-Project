AI Tools

Sales and unit-demand forecasting, analysis, and model configuration

Purpose and goal

AI Tools brings historical Epicor data, analysis, and forecasting into one place. The goal is to give sales and planning teams a forward view of expected revenue and product demand, helping them prepare for seasonal peaks, changes in customer activity, and upcoming production volume.

The toolset is being developed to support the whole process through the browser: prepare data, explore patterns, compare models, produce forecasts, and review their accuracy as actual results arrive. Forecasts will support business decisions alongside the order book and planners' judgement.

What we want to understand

Monthly net sales

What revenue can we expect over the next three months, and which customer groups are driving the change?

Net invoiced sales in GBP, excluding VAT and accounting for credits and returns. Includes revenue from all in-scope products.

Weekly unit demand

How many units are likely to be due to ship over the next twelve weeks, and where are the peaks in demand?

Required ship-by dates provide a production-demand indicator. Full units and cover-only work are reported separately; volume is distinct from labour capacity.

Seasonality and lead time

How does demand change throughout the year, and what does that mean for the time customers wait for their orders?

Seasonal analysis will inform forecasting. A later phase will examine lead time from order receipt to actual shipment.

Forecast results will cover customer groups and the company total, using the existing planning groups by default, with Epicor groups as an alternative.

From data to a useful forecast

  1. Prepare: connect historical sources, classify products, and check data quality.
  2. Explore: examine trends, seasonal patterns, customer activity, and product mix.
  3. Experiment: configure features and models, tune parameters, and compare against simple baselines using historical forecasts.
  4. Forecast: review expected sales and units, prediction intervals, and exportable results.
  5. Monitor: compare forecasts with actuals, track accuracy and bias, and decide when to retrain.

Available tools

Model classification is the first step: define which products count as full units, keep cover-only work separate, and review quantities against one-piece planning. Historical imports, analysis, experiments, forecasts, and monitoring will follow.
